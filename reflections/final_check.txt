What I understand better about algorithms now:
An algorithm is a clear list of steps that solves one specific problem. I learned that every algorithm needs a defined input, a defined output, and ordered steps in between. I also learned that edge cases, like an empty list, must be handled on purpose and not left to chance. Loops and decisions are the two building blocks I used most, for example when counting numbers greater than 10 and searching for a name. Writing pseudocode first helped me plan before thinking about any programming language.

How tracing changed my confidence:
Tracing let me follow my algorithm one step at a time with real values, so I could see exactly how the count changed. When I traced the list [4, 15, 10, 22, 8], I confirmed that 10 is not counted because it is not greater than 10. Seeing the correct result of 2 gave me proof that the logic works. I now feel more confident that my pseudocode will behave correctly before I write any code.

One pseudocode habit I will keep:
